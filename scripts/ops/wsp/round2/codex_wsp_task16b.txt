Part C (new). Tools for analysing WHY an edit works: how much of a working weight change lies in the directions a readable signal provides.
  C1. `tools/wsp_adapter_ops.py project ADAPTER --onto SIGNAL --k K --side out|in|both --out DIR [--complement] [--random-seed S] [--pad-rank R]`.
     For every module present in ADAPTER (dense change D_m = the adapter's scaled B·A): take SIGNAL's dense change for the same module, its top-K left singular vectors U (output side) and top-K right singular vectors V (input side). Result module = P_U D_m (side out), D_m P_V (side in) or P_U D_m P_V (side both), where P_U = U U^T, P_V = V V^T. With --complement the result is D_m minus that projection. With --random-seed, U and V are replaced by orthonormal bases of random K-dimensional subspaces of the same spaces (seeded per module name; SIGNAL is then only used for the module list / shapes). A module missing from SIGNAL: projection result is zero (module dropped), complement result is D_m unchanged. Write the result as an adapter in the same layout the other operations of this file write (exact low-rank factorisation by SVD of the result; rank = numerical rank, uniform padding as the other operations do), with the usual wsp_ops.json receipt that also records per module and in total: squared Frobenius norm of D_m, of the result, and their ratio ("captured" share), plus K / d as the chance level for each side.
  C2. `tools/wsp_adapter_ops.py renorm ADAPTER --mode equal|profile [--profile-from OTHER] [--total-norm T] --out DIR [--pad-rank R]`.
     Keep every module's direction (D_m / ||D_m||_F) and replace its magnitude: mode equal gives every module the same Frobenius norm; mode profile gives module m a norm proportional to ||OTHER's D_m||_F (modules missing from OTHER: fail closed). The total Frobenius norm over all modules is T if given, else the total of ADAPTER. Receipt records the old and new per-module norms and totals.
  C3. `tools/wsp_signal_overlap.py --working W_ADAPTER --out FILE.json [--signal-adapter NAME=DIR ...] [--directions NAME=FILE.json ...] [--k 1 4 16 64]` (CPU, read-only).
     For each working module with dense change D_m (SVD: top singular triplet (u1, s1, v1), singular values s):
       - against each signal adapter with dense change G_m for the same module: cos2_out(K) = || U_G,K^T u1 ||^2 and cos2_in(K) = || V_G,K^T v1 ||^2 for each K; captured_out(K) = ||P_{U_G,K} D_m||_F^2 / ||D_m||_F^2, captured_in(K), captured_both(K); chance levels K / d_out and K / d_in; and the cosine of the two dense matrices <D_m, G_m> / (||D_m|| ||G_m||) with its sign.
       - against each --directions file: a JSON holding residual-stream direction vectors per layer; accept (a) {"vectors": {"<id>": {"<layer>": [floats], ...}, ...}} as written in p1-condition/1 files of the surgery worktree, taking every id, and (b) {"layers": {"<layer>": [floats]}}. Only modules that write into the residual stream are compared (down_proj and o_proj of that layer; the vector length must equal the module's output dimension, else that pair is skipped and counted): cos2 between the unit direction and the top-K output subspace of D_m for each K (|| U_D,K^T d ||^2), the share of D_m's squared norm along the direction (|| d^T D_m ||^2 / ||D_m||_F^2), chance levels K / d_out and 1 / d_out.
     Output: per module rows and summaries (plain mean and squared-norm-weighted mean over modules, overall, by module type, and by layer third 0-13 / 14-27 / 28-41), for every signal, every K. Print a compact table of the overall summaries.
