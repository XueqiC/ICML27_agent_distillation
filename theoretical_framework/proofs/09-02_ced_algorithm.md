# Algorithm 9.2 · Consequence-Calibrated Experience Distillation, CED

*Proof*

[← Framework](../README.md) · [Statement](../statements/09-02_ced_algorithm.md) · [Proof](09-02_ced_algorithm.md) · [Experiments](../experiments/09-02_ced_algorithm.md) · [中文](09-02_ced_algorithm_ZH.md)

**What is proved here.** An algorithm is not a theorem, so what this page establishes is twofold. First, for each of the six steps of CED, which earlier statements force that step: a method that skips it loses the guarantee those statements provide. Second, each of the guarantees (G1)–(G3) follows by applying one cited theorem to the output of one step, (G4) records what is left when the hypotheses of those theorems are not met, and the composed guarantee of [Corollary 8.16](../statements/08-16_composed_guarantee.md#s8-16) is placed relative to them.

**Setting used on this page.**

- *Student and recipe.* A complete learner state $`z=(\theta,o)`$, parameters plus optimizer state ([Definition 1.3](../README.md#s1-3)); a declared recipe (update $`U_t`$, optimizer, horizon $`R`$, reference tail); declared deployment tasks with score $`F_E`$; $`L_E(S)`$ is the learning value of a provider policy $`S`$, and $`L_E(0)`$ that of always using the reference.
- *Evidence.* $`K`$ trusted demonstrations per task family and a budget of $`n`$ permitted experiments (teacher queries at states the student visits, environment branches, resets) ([Definition 1.2](../README.md#s1-2)). $`D`$ is the transcript of all evidence; $`\mathcal R_{\rm info}(D)`$ is its information regret ([Theorem 2.2](../statements/02-02_regret_decomposition_decision_ambiguity.md#s2-2)).
- *Candidate feedback laws.* $`P^0,\dots,P^M`$ emit messages $`Y\sim P^j_t(\cdot\mid z)`$; $`P^0`$ is the reference. A message produces the increment $`\Delta^j`$ of the complete state. The one-boundary response of a message is $`g_{t,z}(Y)`$ ([Definition 1.4](../README.md#s1-4)); $`\bar g_t(z,j)=\mathbb E_{P^j_t}g_{t,z}(Y)`$, and the advantage over the reference is $`A_t(z,j)=\bar g_t(z,j)-\bar g_t(z,0)`$: the change in task value from replacing one round of the reference by candidate $`j`$ at $`z`$, followed by the reference tail.
- *Legal pool and interface.* $`\lbrace y_1,\dots,y_m\rbrace`$ is the finite verified pool; $`\phi(y)\in\mathbb R^r`$ a response interface with coefficient matrix $`C`$ ([Definition 4.8](../statements/04-08_response_operator.md#s4-8)); $`\mathcal M`$ a confidence set for the target feature mean, $`\mathcal K`$ the response outputs reachable by legal mixtures, and $`r(\mathcal M,\mathcal K)`$ the information–compilation radius ([Proposition 6.16](../statements/06-16_information_compilation_radius.md#s6-16)); $`B`$ the density bound of a mixture.
- *Learner-relative weights and coverage.* At agent states $`s`$ the student visits, $`W_s`$ is the learner-relative weight and $`k_s=KH\,d_T(s)`$ the teacher coverage; $`n_s^\star=(\sqrt{W_s/\lambda}-k_s)_+`$ is the water-filling allocation, with the multiplier $`\lambda>0`$ fixed by $`\sum_sn_s^\star=n`$ ([Proposition 6.10](../statements/06-10_water_filling_allocation.md#s6-10)).
- *Magnitude quantities.* Per coordinate, $`\lambda`$ (outside the water-filling formula) is a message's amplitude relative to the running RMS $`\sqrt v`$, $`\rho`$ the signal-to-noise ratio read from the optimizer state, and $`\lambda^\star=(1-\beta_1^{\,T+1})/(\rho\,\omega_T)`$ the value peak of the restricted mean-field model of [Corollary 7.2](../statements/07-02_stationary_amplitude_law.md#s7-2).
- *Paired-replacement score.* At an audited state, $`\hat S^{jk}`$ is the mean over $`r`$ independent replicas of the difference of surrogate outcomes $`\tilde F`$ after one round with $`j`$ and with $`k`$, followed by a shared surrogate tail with common random numbers ([Definition 8.6](../statements/08-06_crn_paired_replacement_score.md#s8-6)). $`e^{jk}_{\rm stat}`$ is its empirical-Bernstein radius and $`(\alpha,\varepsilon_{\rm sur})`$ the surrogate calibration of [Assumption 8.7](../statements/08-07_certificate_assumptions.md#s8-7). The radius is *strict* when its range term uses a range known before the data, and *diagnostic* when it uses the range observed in the same sample.
- *Confidence level* $`\delta`$; *checkpoints* $`t_1<\dots<t_A`$.

**Statement ([Algorithm 9.2](../statements/09-02_ced_algorithm.md#s9-2)).**

*Input:* a student $`z_0`$ with its declared recipe and deployment tasks; $`K`$ trusted demonstrations per family and a budget of $`n`$ permitted experiments; candidate generators for $`P^1,\dots,P^M`$ over a finite legal pool and the reference $`P^0`$; an audit budget (checkpoints, $`r`$ replicas, a surrogate $`\tilde F`$, a few full-tail calibration audits, confidence $`\delta`$). *Output:* the distilled student $`z_R`$; an evidence ledger; a gap report; at each checkpoint the scores $`\hat S^{jk}`$ with strict and diagnostic radii, the selected law and whether the selection is certified; a cost ledger; a frozen selector with separate method-transfer and policy-transfer reports.

*Procedure.*

1. **Record the complete learner state** $`z_t=(\theta_t,o_t)`$ at every checkpoint, including $`m`$, $`v`$, the step counter, the sampler and the random state, and record warmth ([Remark 5.11](../statements/05-11_checking_warm_state_conditions.md#s5-11)).
2. **Acquire before compiling, and route each gap.** Estimate $`W_s`$ and $`k_s`$ by a pilot. Where a calibrated interface exists, compute $`r(\mathcal M,\mathcal K)`$: ignore uncertainty in $`\ker C`$; treat a large diameter of $`C\mathcal M`$ as an information gap (targeted queries); treat a target outside $`\mathcal K`$ as a realizability gap (enlarge the legal pool); treat a summary that is not closed under the declared updates as a closure gap (extend tests or state). Allocate the queries of an information gap by $`n_s^\star=(\sqrt{W_s/\lambda}-k_s)_+`$, in general the c-optimal design with target $`c=\sum_tJ_t^*g_t`$; keep a ledger in which only new evidence counts.
3. **Compile candidates as legal mixtures** $`P^0,\dots,P^M`$ over the pool, each specified by the packets it actually emits, with its density bound $`B`$ and realizability residual reported.
4. **Record each candidate's learner statistic; screen magnitudes:** its mean optimizer increment, clipping activation rate, and per-coordinate $`(\lambda,\rho)`$; never treat equal immediate parameter changes as equivalence; oversized messages generate rescaled or clipped variants, which are new candidates; nothing is selected by the amplitude law.
5. **Measure consequence at the actual states:** compute $`\hat S^{jk}`$ with strict and diagnostic radii for all pairs at $`z_t`$; calibrate $`(\alpha,\varepsilon_{\rm sur})`$ against a few full-tail paired audits; reuse an interface across checkpoints only when its full cost, drift checks included, is below refitting; use two-fidelity estimation only after an independent pilot supports its cost condition; use first-order scores only as a screen on unflagged pairs whose margin exceeds the second-order remainder; fit any response model under the bounded-coefficient objective.
6. **Select, train, re-audit, freeze, test transfer:** select $`j_t\in\arg\max_j[\hat S^{j0}-e^{j0}_{\rm stat}-\varepsilon_{\rm sur}]`$ with the strict radius, keeping the reference if no strict lower bound is positive; train with $`P^{j_t}`$ to the next checkpoint, extending the selection to unaudited states only with transport terms or a re-audit; freeze everything before the evaluation data and report method transfer and policy transfer separately.

*Guarantees.* (G1) Under Assumption 8.7, with probability at least $`1-\delta`$, simultaneously for all pairs at $`z_t`$: a selection $`j_t\ne0`$ with positive strict lower bound does not lower the task value of one round followed by the reference tail. (G2) With full-tail paired audits at the actual states and lower-confidence-bound selection, $`L_E({\rm adaptive})-L_E(0)\ge-\delta`$. (G3) No post-processing step lowers the information regret; under the assumptions of [Theorem 6.5](../statements/06-05_achievability.md#s6-5), step 2 attains the learner-relative information bound up to the constants stated there, and with a finite legal pool the realizability residual of step 3 is added. (G4) Otherwise the claim is empirical. *Composed guarantee:* [Corollary 8.16](../statements/08-16_composed_guarantee.md#s8-16) is valid but vacuous at the measured scale. *Known limitation:* with the a-priori range of Assumption 8.7 the strict radius can exceed typical candidate gaps ([Remark 8.17](../statements/08-17_open_problems.md#s8-17)), and step 6 then falls under (G4).

**Proof.** The argument has two parts: why each step is required, and how each guarantee follows.

*Part 1: why each step is required.*

- **Step 1 (complete state).** The statistic a feedback law must carry depends on the optimizer state, not on $`\theta`$ alone:
  - at a cold Adam step the update is a sign quotient and magnitudes are retained only in $`m`$ ([Proposition 5.3](../statements/05-03_cold_start_adam_sign_quotient.md#s5-3));
  - warm Adam is first-order affine in the consumed gradient with coefficients set by $`(m,v)`$, while cold steps separate equal-mean feedback ([Propositions 5.6](../statements/05-06_warm_adam_first_order_affine.md#s5-6) and [5.7](../statements/05-07_cold_steps_separate_equal_mean.md#s5-7));
  - the first-order statistic is learner-specific ([Theorem 5.10](../statements/05-10_first_order_statistic_by_learner.md#s5-10));
  - two reachable Adam states with the same parameters and different moments have different next steps ([Proposition 7.6](../statements/07-06_equal_immediate_parameters_different_continuation.md#s7-6)).
  
  Coverage and transport are statements about complete states ([Theorem 8.2](../statements/08-02_frozen_finite_interface_selection_transport.md#s8-2): the transport bound is indexed by the complete state $`z\in\mathcal C_t`$). A checkpoint that keeps only $`\theta`$ cannot be audited.
- **Step 2 (acquire before compiling; route each gap).**
  - Post-processing cannot reduce information regret ([Proposition 2.4](../statements/02-04_post_processing_cannot_reduce_information.md#s2-4): $`\mathcal R_{\rm info}(C(D))\ge\mathcal R_{\rm info}(D)`$ for every randomized map $`C`$); under aliasing, replay has zero value ([Corollary 6.7](../statements/06-07_aliasing_zero_value_replay.md#s6-7): two environments with the same demonstration law and different learner-relative functionals cannot be told apart by any distiller).
  - Passive data can leave a gap that only queries close ([Theorem 2.6](../statements/02-06_passive_impossibility_active_attainability.md#s2-6): worst-case regret at least $`\Delta`$ without queries, attainable with them).
  - Demonstrations and queries share one budget at a learner-relative exchange rate ([Proposition 2.11](../statements/02-11_shared_information_budget.md#s2-11), [Corollary 6.8](../statements/06-08_exchange_rate_between_demonstrations_queries.md#s6-8)).
  - The minimal error is of order $`\mathfrak n_{K,n}`$ ([Theorem 6.3](../statements/06-03_learner_relative_lower_bound.md#s6-3)) and is attained by the c-optimal design ([Theorem 6.5](../statements/06-05_achievability.md#s6-5)), which exists also when individual designs leave nuisance directions unidentified ([Lemma 6.4](../statements/06-04_design_minimax_equality.md#s6-4)) and which in the state-coverage model is water-filling ([Proposition 6.10](../statements/06-10_water_filling_allocation.md#s6-10)).
  - *Routing.* For a fixed interface, the radius $`r(\mathcal M,\mathcal K)`$ is at least half the diameter of $`C\mathcal M`$ and at least the largest distance from $`C\mathcal M`$ to $`\mathcal K`$, and it is zero only when both vanish ([Proposition 6.16](../statements/06-16_information_compilation_radius.md#s6-16)). Hence: shrinking $`\mathcal M`$ along $`\ker C`$ changes neither lower bound, so queries spent there buy nothing on the declared tests; when the distance term dominates, no amount of information about the same target lowers the radius below it, so only a larger pool (a larger $`\mathcal K`$) helps; when the diameter term dominates, information is what is missing. A summary that predicts the current tests but is not closed under the declared updates can leave a future error of size 1 with zero current error ([Proposition 4.11](../statements/04-11_static_not_dynamic_closure_error.md#s4-11)), and no query about the current tests detects it; the remedy is to extend the tests or the recorded state.
  
  So queries must be acquired, and allocated by the learner's weights, before any compilation, and only for gaps that information can close: no later step can recover what the transcript lacks, and no query can supply what the legal pool cannot express.
- **Step 3 (legal mixtures and packets).**
  - A target feature mean is realized by a legal mixture exactly when it lies in the convex hull of the pool's feature vectors, then with support at most $`r+1`$; sparse realizations can inflate the density bound $`B`$, which enters every spectral error bound ([Proposition 6.14](../statements/06-14_convex_realizability_legal_pool.md#s6-14), [Theorem 4.9](../statements/04-09_spectral_sufficiency.md#s4-9)). Hence $`B`$ and the realizability residual are reported, and the oracle law of [Theorem 6.5](../statements/06-05_achievability.md#s6-5) is emitted only when it can be generated.
  - Grouping changes the outcome exactly through the non-additive part of the joint-packet response ([Proposition 9.1](../statements/09-01_coupling_width.md#s9-1): the coupling width is zero if and only if the packet response is additive). A candidate is therefore its packet law, not a bag of units.
- **Step 4 (learner statistic and magnitude screen).**
  - Candidates with the same mean gradient can differ at first order ([Theorem 5.10](../statements/05-10_first_order_statistic_by_learner.md#s5-10)), and candidates with the same immediate parameter change can differ in their continuation ([Proposition 7.6](../statements/07-06_equal_immediate_parameters_different_continuation.md#s7-6)).
  - In the restricted mean-field model of [Corollary 7.2](../statements/07-02_stationary_amplitude_law.md#s7-2), a larger same-direction message can be worth less; parameter-only and cosine scores misrank or tie in that regime ([Proposition 7.3](../statements/07-03_inconsistency_two_proxy_classes.md#s7-3)), and first-order scores are unreliable where the second-moment channel dominates ([Remark 7.5](../statements/07-05_where_first_order_rankers_fail.md#s7-5)). This makes magnitude a reason to *generate* variants for measurement.
  - It is not a reason to select or reject: the model's real-model prediction had the opposite sign to the measured responses ([Check 7.6](../experiments/07-02_stationary_amplitude_law.md#c7-6)), so its premises cannot be assumed at the actual state.
  - Global-norm clipping caps $`\lambda`$ ([Remark 7.4](../statements/07-04_relation_prior_work_role_clipping.md#s7-4)), so a clipped variant is a genuinely different candidate.
  
  Flagged candidates and their variants are therefore ranked only by the finite measurement of step 5.
- **Step 5 (measure at the actual states).**
  - The paired-replacement score is exact in expectation, with no first-order approximation and no curvature constant ([Theorem 8.8](../statements/08-08_crn_paired_replacement_certificate.md#s8-8)(a)); under common random numbers its variance is governed by the difference of the two candidates' increments ([Proposition 8.10](../statements/08-10_cost_and_crn.md#s8-10)(c)).
  - The certificate of Theorem 8.8 requires a range known before the data ([Assumption 8.7](../statements/08-07_certificate_assumptions.md#s8-7)); a radius built from the observed range has no such guarantee. Hence the two radii are reported separately and only the strict one enters selection.
  - The surrogate differs from the task objective, so $`(\alpha,\varepsilon_{\rm sur})`$ must be calibrated against full-tail task audits ([Assumption 8.7](../statements/08-07_certificate_assumptions.md#s8-7)(A), [Theorem 8.3](../statements/08-03_actual_state_adaptive_audit_fidelity.md#s8-3), [Remark 8.11](../statements/08-11_surrogate_calibration_and_transfer.md#s8-11)).
  - *Reuse.* Shared message features carry an averaged error that is a singular-value tail sum, not the operator norm ([Proposition 8.13](../statements/08-13_shared_features_across_checkpoints.md#s8-13)), and transferring an anchor's interface costs its estimation error plus the measured operator drift ([Proposition 8.14](../statements/08-14_anchor_reuse_drift_certificate.md#s8-14)). Both terms must be paid, and the drift check itself needs paired continuations; reuse saves compute only if those continuations cost less than refitting.
  - *Two-fidelity estimation.* The two-fidelity estimator is unbiased, and it is cheaper than full continuations exactly when the cost condition of [Proposition 8.15](../statements/08-15_two_fidelity_response_estimation.md#s8-15) holds. That condition involves unknown variances and a correlation that has not been measured on the real model, so it must be supported by an independent pilot, whose cost counts.
  - A first-order score certifies a pair only when its first-order margin exceeds the second-order remainder ([Corollary 8.9](../statements/08-09_first_order_version_margin_criterion.md#s8-9)), hence the restriction of the screen.
  - For a response model on features, the bounded-coefficient objective is the exact certified unresolved discrepancy ([Theorem 8.1](../statements/08-01_stability_approximation_tradeoff.md#s8-1)), so a model with smaller residual but larger coefficients gives a worse certificate.
- **Step 6 (select, re-audit, freeze, transfer).**
  - Selecting by the strict lower bound is the decision rule of [Theorem 8.8](../statements/08-08_crn_paired_replacement_certificate.md#s8-8)(c); selecting by point estimates or diagnostic radii carries no such guarantee.
  - Dropping the transport term is invalid: there are instances where an audit reports zero discrepancy while the learning gap is $`1`$ ([Theorem 8.2](../statements/08-02_frozen_finite_interface_selection_transport.md#s8-2)(f)). Hence the transport terms or the re-audit for unaudited states.
  - The audit event of [Theorem 8.2](../statements/08-02_frozen_finite_interface_selection_transport.md#s8-2)(a) is simultaneous over a candidate set fixed before the audit, so everything must be frozen before the data that evaluates it.
  - Method transfer and policy transfer are different functionals with different test classes ([Theorem 4.2](../statements/04-02_sufficiency_duality_transfer.md#s4-2)(c): transfer holds only through a verified decomposition of the target responses), so they are reported separately.

*Part 2: the guarantees.*

**(G1).** At checkpoint $`z_t`$, step 5 computes $`\hat S^{jk}`$ and the strict radii $`e^{jk}_{\rm stat}`$ for all ordered pairs under the hypotheses of Assumption 8.7. By [Theorem 8.8](../statements/08-08_crn_paired_replacement_certificate.md#s8-8)(b), with probability at least $`1-\delta`$, simultaneously for all pairs,

```math
L_F(P^j)-L_F(P^0)\ \ge\ \alpha\big(\hat S^{j0}-e^{j0}_{\rm stat}-\varepsilon_{\rm sur}\big).
```

Step 6 selects $`j_t\ne0`$ only when the bracket is positive, and $`\alpha>0`$, so on this event $`L_F(P^{j_t})-L_F(P^0)>0`$. By [Definition 8.6](../statements/08-06_crn_paired_replacement_score.md#s8-6), $`L_F(P^j)-L_F(P^0)=A_t(z_t,j)`$, the task-value change of replacing one round of the reference by $`P^j`$ at $`z_t`$ followed by the reference tail. This is (G1). Because the event is simultaneous over all pairs, it holds for the data-dependent choice $`j_t`$. A diagnostic radius does not satisfy the range hypothesis of Assumption 8.7, so a selection made with it is outside (G1).

**(G2).** If the audits of step 5 are full-tail paired audits at the states training actually reaches and step 6 selects by their lower confidence bound (keeping the reference otherwise), the resulting provider policy is the adaptive policy of [Theorem 8.3](../statements/08-03_actual_state_adaptive_audit_fidelity.md#s8-3)(c). Part (c)(ii) of that theorem gives, unconditionally over audits and training, $`L_E({\rm adaptive})-L_E(0)\ge-\delta`$. The mechanism is the signed identity of Theorem 8.3(b), $`L_E(S)-L_E(0)=\sum_t\mathbb E_{d^S_t}A_t(Z_t,J_t)`$: the whole-run gain is the sum of per-round advantages at the states actually reached, each of which the audit controls.

**(G3).** Steps 3–6 take the transcript $`D`$ produced by step 2 and transform it (replay, rewriting, mixing, packing, selection), so the student they produce is a randomized post-processing $`C(D)`$. By [Proposition 2.4](../statements/02-04_post_processing_cannot_reduce_information.md#s2-4), $`\mathcal R_{\rm info}(C(D))\ge\mathcal R_{\rm info}(D)`$: none of these steps lowers the information regret. The acquisition of step 2 is the c-optimal design with target $`c=\sum_tJ_t^*g_t`$, which under the assumptions of [Theorem 6.5](../statements/06-05_achievability.md#s6-5) attains the learner-relative information bound up to the constant factors stated there. One of those assumptions is that the oracle law at the estimate is emitted. When step 3 can only mix a finite legal pool, the emitted law differs from it by the realizability residual of [Proposition 6.14](../statements/06-14_convex_realizability_legal_pool.md#s6-14), and by the triangle inequality this residual, transferred through the interface, adds to the bound.

**(G4).** Each of the cases listed in (G4) removes a hypothesis used above: point-estimate or diagnostic-radius selection removes the strict lower-bound condition used in (G1)–(G2); an unverified calibration removes Assumption 8.7(A); use at unaudited states without transport terms removes the transport hypothesis of Theorem 8.2, and Theorem 8.2(f) shows that the conclusion can then fail. No certificate remains, and the claim is about the mean outcome of the frozen method over a declared population, supported by whole-run randomized comparisons.

**Composed guarantee.** [Corollary 8.16](../statements/08-16_composed_guarantee.md#s8-16) assembles the anchor reuse bound, the two-fidelity estimation error and the realizability radius into one bound on the whole-run gap, through the stopped decomposition with an approximate value ([Proposition 3.4](../statements/03-04_joint_decomposition_approximate_value.md#s3-4)). Its per-step terms are absolute precisions from bounded-outcome concentration: they do not shrink with the size of the per-step effect, while the decomposition sums them over steps. At the measured evaluation noise and affordable replicate counts the per-step floor already exceeds the score range (Corollary 8.16 and its experiments page report the numbers). The bound is therefore valid but vacuous at that scale, and it does not lift steps 5–6 out of (G4). This statement concerns the estimators and inequalities used there; it is not a proof that certifying continued-training sufficiency is impossible.

**Known limitation.** Under Assumption 8.7(B) the range $`B`$ is a-priori ($`B\le2`$ for $`\tilde F\in[0,1]`$), and the range term $`7B\log(4M^2/\delta)/(3(r-1))`$ of $`e^{jk}_{\rm stat}`$ can exceed the gaps between nearby candidates for any affordable $`r`$. On the real model with $`r=64`$ replicas, the first candidate pair has a score of $`+0.0014`$, a strict radius of $`0.325`$ and a diagnostic radius of $`0.0012`$ ([Check 8.17](../experiments/08-08_crn_paired_replacement_certificate.md#c8-17)). Then no strict lower bound is positive, step 6 keeps the reference or reports an uncertified selection, and the run falls under (G4) until a provable range much smaller than the a-priori one is available ([Remark 8.17](../statements/08-17_open_problems.md#s8-17)). $`\square`$

## Related work

- **Data and teacher selection.** LESS ([Xia et al., 2024](#ref-xia2024less)) ranks training data by gradient cosine similarity to a target; GRACE ([Panigrahi et al., 2025](#ref-panigrahi2025good)) scores teachers for distillation. Algorithm 9.2 instead ranks candidate feedback laws by their measured consequence at the actual optimizer state, and [Proposition 7.3](../statements/07-03_inconsistency_two_proxy_classes.md#s7-3) shows where cosine-type scores are inconsistent in a restricted model.
- **Active acquisition.** DAgger ([Ross et al., 2011](#ref-ross2011reduction)) queries the teacher at the student's states. Step 2 allocates such queries by learner-relative weights rather than uniformly ([Proposition 6.10](../statements/06-10_water_filling_allocation.md#s6-10)), and only where the gap is one of information.
- **Influence and metagradients.** TracIn ([Pruthi et al., 2020](#ref-pruthi2020estimating)), influence functions ([Koh & Liang, 2017](#ref-koh2017understanding)), metagradients ([Engstrom et al., 2025](#ref-engstrom2025optimizing)) and In-Run Data Shapley ([Wang et al., 2025](#ref-wang2025data)) are first-order or fixed-state attributions. In Algorithm 9.2 they can serve only as the screen of step 5, under [Corollary 8.9](../statements/08-09_first_order_version_margin_criterion.md#s8-9).
- **Multifidelity estimation.** The two-fidelity option of step 5 applies the classical control-variate idea of multifidelity Monte Carlo ([Peherstorfer, Willcox & Gunzburger, 2016](#ref-peherstorfer2016optimal)) to continuation responses.
